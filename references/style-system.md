# Visual system

Use this reference for **commercial mode** when designing a new image family, selecting a composition, or correcting an image that looks like generic AI art. For requested apsara portraits, use [Apsara mode](apsara-mode.md); commercial product stages, material quotas, and religious-figure exclusions do not apply there.

## Core idea

The style combines two worlds:

- Dunhuang material culture supplies mineral colors, cave darkness, wall patina, ribbons, lotus geometry, caisson order, mountain silhouettes, and luminous mural surfaces.
- Modern commercial art direction supplies product staging, device mockups, controlled studio light, asymmetry, depth, clear hierarchy, and advertising-level finish.

The fusion must remain visible. If the scene is only an old mural, the commercial layer is missing. If it is only a luxury product photo with a lotus pasted behind it, the Dunhuang layer is superficial.

## Color architecture

### Dominant base

Charcoal cave black occupies 35% to 55% of the image. It creates depth and gives mineral colors room to glow. Use textured near-black rather than flat pure black.

Suggested range: `#15120F` to `#26201A`.

### Illuminated surface

Sand mineral white occupies 20% to 35%, mainly in mural panels, packaging, reflected floor light, and distant openings.

Suggested range: `#D8C39C` to `#F0DFC0`.

### Flow colors

- Cinnabar: `#8F3828` to `#B85435`.
- Malachite: `#2E6D63` to `#4C8474`.
- Lapis: `#28577B` to `#376D8D`.

Use the three colors in one connected ribbon system. Do not create separate, unrelated squiggles in every corner.

### Gold

Ochre gold occupies 5% to 12%. It belongs in a sun disc, fine caisson edge, bottle cap, reflected light, or glowing architectural detail.

Suggested range: `#A66B2A` to `#D09B45`.

Do not wash the whole frame in gold. Excess gold destroys the mineral-color contrast and turns the result into generic luxury advertising.

## Materials

Use at least three material families in a hero image:

- Weathered cave rock with irregular matte absorption.
- Cracked mural plaster with mineral-pigment staining.
- Reflective dark stone or shallow water for depth and light continuity.
- Matte ceramic for vessels.
- Frosted or satin glass for cosmetics.
- Brushed bronze for caps and fine architectural edges.

Material descriptions must affect light. Ceramic needs soft diffuse highlights; glass needs continuous reflections and internal depth; bronze needs controlled specular edges; rock needs broken, low-gloss highlights.

## Motif grammar

### Ribbons

Ribbons are the main motion device. They should:

- enter from one frame edge;
- curve around or behind focal subjects;
- cross the middle depth;
- exit or dissolve near another edge;
- vary in width without becoming spaghetti-like;
- follow one direction of travel.

Never let a ribbon cover the product label, cut through a face, or create a tangent at the edge of a phone.

### Lotus

Use one foreground lotus cluster or one graphic lotus disc. Multiple scattered lotus flowers read as decorative filler.

### Caisson geometry

Use as a partial border, distant ceiling detail, circular disc, or high-right architectural anchor. Avoid full-frame ornate borders unless the brief is explicitly poster-like.

### Mountains and caves

Mountains create painted depth. Caves create architectural depth. Keep at least one layer physically realistic enough to support the product stage.

## Composition families

### Dual-anchor panorama

Best for a text-free 5:2 cover.

- Left anchor: ceramic vessel, sculptural artifact, image-generation console, or layered art panels.
- Right anchor: product bottle, device, packaging, or another commercial object.
- Middle path: water, reflective floor, canyon opening, ribbon flow, or light corridor.
- Background: mural wall, cave architecture, waterfall, or mountain aperture.

Avoid mirroring the two anchors. They should balance by visual weight, not identical shape.

### Asymmetric product stage

Best for product ads.

- Product sits between 58% and 72% of frame width.
- One large mural panel sits behind it.
- A smaller panel or prop sits farther back.
- The opposite third stays calm enough for copy or contextual texture.

### Mural gateway

Best for cultural events or editorial covers.

- One luminous painted panel acts as a portal.
- Foreground rocks and ribbons create approach.
- A small product or figure supplies scale.
- Keep the portal off-center.

### Layered visual-production studio

Best for AI image, design, and creative-tool topics.

- Use several image panels at varied depths and aspect ratios.
- Include one product or device to prove commercial application.
- Keep panels image-led. Do not fill them with fake text or dashboards.
- Use lighting to separate each plane.

### Text-led split cover

Best when exact copy must be generated into the image.

- Quiet title zone occupies 38% to 48%.
- Visual world occupies the rest.
- Texture may continue behind the title, but no high-contrast object should compete with it.
- Use two lines and one highlighted phrase at most.

## Lighting

Default light scheme:

- warm directional key light from upper left or a mural portal;
- narrow warm rim light on product edges;
- soft reflected gold on the floor;
- deep but readable cave shadows;
- light haze only where it reveals depth.

Do not add glow around every object. One luminous origin is enough. Other highlights must be reflections or secondary bounce.

## Depth and scale

A convincing scene contains:

1. Foreground: rock, lotus, ribbon edge, pedestal, vessel, or product.
2. Middle depth: main mural panel, commercial object, device, or display frame.
3. Background: cave arch, distant mountain, waterfall, architectural wall, or sun disc.

Use partial occlusion to prove depth, but never hide the focal object's defining silhouette.

## Text integration

When text is required:

- use exact copy supplied by the user;
- render it once;
- use one Chinese sans-serif family;
- keep the headline to two lines when possible;
- highlight one numeric or commercial phrase with ochre gold;
- prohibit subtitles, filler labels, seals, and device microcopy unless explicitly supplied.

When no text is required, explicitly ban letters and numbers on product labels, phone screens, panels, architecture, seals, and watermarks.

## Commercial object rules

### Bottles

- Keep cap, neck, shoulder, body, and base aligned.
- Show a consistent material boundary.
- Use a contact shadow at the base.
- Avoid duplicate reflections that look like a second bottle.

### Ceramic vessels

- Keep the ellipse of the mouth consistent with camera height.
- Pattern must wrap with the vessel curvature.
- Do not paste flat mural art across the silhouette.

### Phones

- Keep vertical edges parallel unless perspective is deliberate.
- Use a believable bezel and screen reflection.
- Prefer image-led content when exact interface copy is absent.

### Image panels

- Use varied sizes and depths.
- Avoid four equal floating rectangles.
- Give panels physical thickness or a clear luminous plane.

## Anti-slop rules

Reject or revise outputs with:

- generic purple-blue AI glow;
- robot heads, circuit brains, or holographic code rain;
- symmetrical temple corridors with a product dead center;
- three equal cards across the frame;
- gold on every surface;
- random lotus flowers in every empty area;
- unreadable pseudo-Chinese text;
- real-world brand marks invented by the model;
- ornate tourist-poster borders;
- fantasy-game armor, magic runes, or RPG splash-screen lighting;
- religious figures introduced as generic decoration;
- flat collages with no consistent perspective or light source.

## Density control

For text-free images, add detail across the full width but preserve hierarchy:

- one primary object;
- one secondary anchor on the opposite side;
- two to four middle-depth panels or architectural features;
- one continuous ribbon system;
- one lotus occurrence;
- one clear light origin.

More objects do not automatically create a richer image. Increase material detail and depth before increasing object count.
